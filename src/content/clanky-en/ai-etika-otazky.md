# AI and Ethics - 5 Key Questions

Artificial intelligence is transforming the world, but it also brings serious ethical questions to the fore. Which ones are the most critical?

## 1. Who is responsible for AI decisions?

When an AI makes a mistake, who bears the responsibility?
- The algorithm developer?
- The company deploying the AI?
- The user?

### Example
An autonomous vehicle causes an accident. Who is at fault?

### Solution
We need clear legal frameworks and regulations.

## 2. How can we ensure AI fairness?

AI systems can be **biased**—showing prejudice against certain groups.

### Problems
- Discrimination in hiring processes
- Unfair credit scoring
- Racial bias in the justice system

### Causes
- Training data contains inherent bias
- Algorithms amplify existing inequalities

### Solution
- Diverse development teams
- Auditing AI systems
- Algorithmic transparency

## 3. What about privacy protection?

AI requires data. Massive amounts of data. Often, personal data.

### Risks
- Surveillance and profiling
- Misuse of personal information
- Loss of anonymity

### Questions
- Who owns the data?
- How long is it retained?
- Can I request its deletion?

### Solution
- GDPR and similar regulations
- Privacy by design
- The right to be forgotten

## 4. Will AI replace human labor?

Automation could lead to mass unemployment.

### At-risk professions
- Routine administrative tasks
- Basic copywriting
- Data entry
- Simple programming

### Emerging professions
- AI trainers
- Prompt engineers
- AI ethics officers

### Solution
- Reskilling programs
- Lifelong learning
- Social safety nets

## 5. What happens if AI surpasses human intelligence?

**AGI** (Artificial General Intelligence)—AI that is more intelligent than a human.

### Risks
- Loss of control
- Existential risk
- Unforeseen consequences

### Expert opinions
- **Optimists**: AGI will solve all our problems
- **Pessimists**: AGI poses an existential risk
- **Realists**: We must proceed with caution

### Solution
- AI safety research
- International cooperation
- Ethical standards

## What can we do?

### As individuals
1. **Educate yourself** — understand how AI works
2. **Be critical** — do not believe everything blindly
3. **Protect your privacy** — minimize data sharing
4. **Use it responsibly** — AI is a tool

### As a society
1. **Regulation** — clear rules of engagement
2. **Transparency** — open algorithms
3. **Education** — AI literacy for everyone
4. **Dialogue** — public debate regarding AI

## Real-world examples

### Positive
- AI diagnostics saving lives
- Personalized education
- More efficient scientific research

### Negative
- Deepfakes and disinformation
- Mass surveillance
- Autonomous weapons

## Conclusion

AI is neither good nor evil—it depends on how we use it. We must address these ethical questions **now**, not when it is already too late.

The keys are:
- **Transparency** — knowing how AI works
- **Accountability** — ensuring someone is responsible for decisions
- **Fairness** — AI serving everyone, not just a few
- **Safety** — keeping AI under control
